\begin{abstract}
For a symmetric zero-diagonal sign matrix \(A\), put
\[
 M(A)=\max_{x\in\eps^n}\left|\frac12x^{\mathsf T}Ax\right|,
 \qquad F(n)=\min_A M(A).
\]
The open question is whether \(F(n)/n^{3/2}\) converges.  We first audit the
previous bounds \(1/\pi\le\liminf\le\limsup\le1/2\), the augmented cut-code
identity, and one-vertex monotonicity; all survive audit.  We then show that
the proposed cube relaxation has the unique optimizer zero at zero temperature
and at every fixed unscaled positive temperature, with a leading-order
zero-temperature integrality gap.  In contrast, an elliptope relaxation has
the exact thermodynamic limit \(1/2\), while the defined two-covariance,
edgewise-oriented Gaussian certificate has asymptotic lower-bound constant
\(1/\pi\).
We identify the augmented cut code as a binary linear code whose dual consists
of even-cardinality Eulerian subgraphs, and derive an exact signed-Eulerian
MacWilliams formula.  Ordinary graphons and limiting empirical spectra are
proved to erase the \(n^{3/2}\) functional.  Exact computer-assisted
computation gives
\(F(2),\ldots,F(12)=1,3,4,4,5,9,10,12,13,17,18\).  Exact cross-block computation
shows that optimal blocks of orders 2 and 8 cannot occur together in an
order-10 optimum.  We also identify the one-vertex Bellman state as an exact
energy-weighted projective covering radius and certify that the code of exact
maximizers, and even the complete scalar partition-function curve, can be
insufficient.  Retaining the nonlinear arcsine term gives a trace-four bound;
a parity theorem for its Gram defect yields $F(20)\ge30$ and $F(21)\ge32$.
The fully optimized one-parameter certificate nevertheless has exact
asymptotic constant $1/\pi$.  We derive an exact multivertex weighted Bellman
identity, then prove that its weighted-entropy union-bound target has an
unavoidable leading constant gap.  The replacement is an exact switching
orbit noncoverage problem on the projective rank-one group.  A complete
order-nine catalogue shows that even the energy histogram plus the two-point
overlap law of exact maximizers does not determine extension, while the
order-ten catalogue exhibits three exact finite-temperature optimizer phases.
The annealed-normalized negative replica is exactly supermultiplicative;
reverse hypercontractivity follows the required extensive parameter curve but
in the wrong direction, isolating a power-saving transport conjecture whose
finite defect remains visible through order nine.  We also record the known
regular Paley construction: square-order Paley conference matrices have
Boolean eigenvectors attaining their spectral ceiling, a statement about
those matrices rather than the minimax \(F\).
Finally, an explicit scalar countermodel shows that even uniform
$O(\sqrt n)$ Bellman control does not force convergence.  The sharp remaining
target is a quenched minimax free-energy limit, or a genuinely
dependence-sensitive composition theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem, audited baseline, and scope}
\label{q:main}

The question, asked by Paata Ivanisvili on MathOverflow in 2022
\cite{mo}, is whether
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{F(n)}{n^{3/2}}
\]
exists.  Here \(A_{ii}=0\), \(A_{ij}=A_{ji}=a_{ij}\in\eps\), and
\[
 Q_A(x)=\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}x_ix_j=\frac12x^{\mathsf T}Ax.
\]

The earlier attempt was rechecked from source rather than assumed.  Its
central conclusions are correct:
\begin{equation}
 \frac1\pi
 \le \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{F(n)}{n^{3/2}}
 \le \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{F(n)}{n^{3/2}}
 \le\frac12,
 \label{eq:baseline}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 F(n)=\binom n2-2\rho(D_n),
 \qquad
 F(n)\le F(n+1)\le F(n)+n.
 \label{eq:oldfacts}
\end{equation}
The lower bound uses two Gaussian sign roundings and the arcsine law.  The
upper bound uses symmetric Paley conference matrices and principal
submatrices.  The code \(D_n\) is the cut-character set augmented by its
negative.  A claim-by-claim audit, including two attribution errors and a
factor-of-two prose correction, is in \texttt{AUDIT.md}.  Nothing in
\eqref{eq:baseline} or \eqref{eq:oldfacts} implies convergence.

All new statements below distinguish a proved identity from a proposed limit
theorem.  Exact computations guide the search but are not used as asymptotic
proofs.

\section{The relaxed-coupling program collapses}

Let \(m=\binom n2\), let \(c_x=(x_ix_j)_{i<j}\), and for arbitrary real
couplings define
\[
 G_n(a)=\max_{x\in\eps^n}|\langle a,c_x\rangle|.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Cube relaxation]
\label{thm:cube}
The function \(G_n\) is a norm on \(\mathbb R^m\).  Hence
\[
 \min_{a\in[-1,1]^m}G_n(a)=0,
\]
and the unique minimizer is \(a=0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Homogeneity and the triangle inequality follow from the maximum of absolute
linear forms.  If \(X\) is uniform on \(\eps^n\), orthogonality of distinct
degree-two Walsh characters gives
\[
 \E\langle a,c_X\rangle^2=\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}^2.
\]
Thus \(G_n(a)=0\) forces \(a=0\).  Since the origin belongs to the cube, the
last assertion follows.
\end{proof}

In particular, any convex feasible set in the original coupling coordinates
that contains every sign coupling contains their convex hull, the whole cube,
and therefore contains this zero optimizer when the objective remains \(G_n\).
By the audited lower bound, its additive integrality gap is at least
\(n\sqrt{n-1}/\pi\), of the full target order.

The same collapse persists at positive temperature.  Retain duplicated
states \((x,s)\in\eps^n\times\eps\) and put
\[
 L_{n,t}(a)=\frac1t\log\sum_{x,s}
 \exp\bigl(t\langle a,s c_x\rangle\bigr),\qquad t>0.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Finite-temperature cube dual]
\label{thm:cubedual}
The function \(L_{n,t}\) is strictly convex and has the unique cube minimizer
zero, with value
\[
 \min_{a\in[-1,1]^m}L_{n,t}(a)=\frac{(n+1)\log2}{t}.
\]
It has the exact dual representation
\[
 \max_{p\in\Delta(\eps^n\times\eps)}
 \left\{
  \frac{H(p)}t-\left\|\E_p[s c_x]\right\|_1
 \right\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Hessian of \(L_{n,t}\) is \(t\) times the covariance matrix of \(s c_x\)
under a full-support Gibbs distribution.  If a linear functional of these
vectors were constant, replacing \(s\) by \(-s\) would make the constant zero;
Walsh orthogonality would then make the functional zero.  The Hessian is
therefore positive definite.  Symmetry makes the gradient vanish at zero,
where the value is \(t^{-1}\log 2^{n+1}\).

The entropy variational formula for log-sum-exp gives
\[
 L_{n,t}(a)=\max_p\left\{
 \langle a,\E_p[s c_x]\rangle+\frac{H(p)}t\right\}.
\]
Both domains are compact and convex and the displayed function is convex in
 \(a\) and concave in \(p\), so Sion's theorem applies.  Minimization of a
linear form over the cube is minus the \(\ell^1\)-norm of its coefficient.
\end{proof}

Thus the simplest probability-measure dual is exact but vacuous for the
discrete problem: the uniform state measure cancels every first moment.

There is nevertheless a useful conditional rounding statement.

\begin{proposition}[Variance-sensitive sign rounding]
\label{prop:rounding}
For \(a\in[-1,1]^m\), set \(V(a)=\sum_e(1-a_e^2)\).  There is a sign vector
\(\sigma\in\eps^m\) for which
\[
 G_n(\sigma)\le G_n(a)
 +\sqrt{2V(a)(n+1)\log2}
 +\frac43(n+1)\log2.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Round the coordinates independently with \(\E\sigma_e=a_e\).  For fixed
\(x\), the centered error
\(\sum_e(\sigma_e-a_e)c_x(e)\) has variance \(V(a)\) and summands bounded by
two.  The two-sided Bernstein bound with parameter \(u\) is at most
\(2e^{-u}\) beyond
\(\sqrt{2V(a)u}+4u/3\).  There are \(2^{n-1}\) distinct cut characters.
Taking \(u=(n+1)\log2\) makes the union failure probability at most \(1/2\).
For a successful outcome, add this uniform error to \(G_n(a)\).
\end{proof}

This gives \(o(n^{3/2})\) loss if \(V(a)=o(n^2)\), but the cube optimizer has
\(V(0)=m\).  The obvious spherical repairs also fail.  On
\(\|a\|_2=\sqrt m\), Parseval gives \(G_n(a)\ge\sqrt m\), attained by a
one-coordinate vector.  Adding \(|a_e|\le1\) to that sphere forces every
\(|a_e|=1\), recovering the original discrete problem exactly.

\section{Temperature scaling and the cross-block wall}

The functional proposed in the question is
\[
 \Phi_{n,\beta}=\min_A\frac1{\beta n^{3/2}}
 \log\sum_x e^{\beta|Q_A(x)|}.
\]
For every fixed \(\beta>0\), the maximum-versus-log-sum-exp inequality gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{F(n)}{n^{3/2}}
 \le\Phi_{n,\beta}
 \le\frac{F(n)}{n^{3/2}}+\frac{\log2}{\beta\sqrt n}.
 \label{eq:proposedtemp}
\end{equation}
Thus existence of \(\lim_n\Phi_{n,\beta}\) for even one fixed positive
\(\beta\) is equivalent to the original limit.  At this normalization,
fixed temperature is already asymptotically zero temperature.

A genuinely extensive mean-field functional is
\[
 \Psi_{n,\beta}=\min_A\frac1{\beta n}
 \log\sum_{x,s}\exp\left(\frac{\beta sQ_A(x)}{\sqrt n}\right).
\]
It satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \frac{F(n)}{n^{3/2}}
 \le\Psi_{n,\beta}
 \le\frac{F(n)}{n^{3/2}}+\frac{(n+1)\log2}{\beta n}.
 \label{eq:extensivesandwich}
\end{equation}
Consequently, existence of \(\lim_n\Psi_{n,\beta}\) for arbitrarily large
fixed \(\beta\) would settle the problem: the limsup-minus-liminf gap would
be at most \(\log2/\beta\) for every such \(\beta\).

The direct interpolation does not have the needed accuracy.  Define
\[
 h_n(t)=\min_A\log\sum_{x,s}e^{tsQ_A(x)}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Exact annealed block estimate]
\label{prop:blocktemp}
For all \(n,k\) and \(t>0\),
\[
 h_{n+k}(t)\le h_n(t)+h_k(t)-\log2+nk\log\cosh t.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a within-block matrix \(A\), write
\(Z_A^s=\sum_xe^{tsQ_A(x)}\).  Replacing all coefficients of the second
block by their negatives swaps its two one-sided partition sums.  Of the two
possible alignments, one satisfies
\[
 Z_A^+Z_B^+ + Z_A^-Z_B^-
 \le\frac12(Z_A^++Z_A^-)(Z_B^++Z_B^-).
\]
Choose every cross coefficient independently and uniformly.  For fixed
\(x,y,s\), averaging the cross exponential gives \((\cosh t)^{nk}\).
Therefore the expected augmented partition sum is at most the right side
above times this factor, and some deterministic cross signing is no larger.
Take logarithms and infima.
\end{proof}

At \(t=\beta/\sqrt{n+k}\) and \(n/(n+k)\to\alpha\), the normalized cross cost
tends to
\[
 \frac\beta2\alpha(1-\alpha),
\]
which is nonzero for fixed \(\beta\) and grows in the zero-temperature limit.
The direct annealed cross block is therefore a leading term, not an error.

Finally, the absolute value cannot be silently removed.  If every edge from
vertex \(1\) is positive and every other edge negative, fix \(x_1=1\) and put
\(T=\sum_{i>1}x_i\).  Then
\[
 Q_A(x)=\frac n2-\frac{(T-1)^2}{2}.
\]
Hence \(\max_xQ_A(x)=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), whereas
\(\max_x|Q_A(x)|=\binom n2\).

\section{Covariance relaxations: two exact asymptotic limits}

Let
\[
 \cE_n=\{R\succeq0:\diag R=\mathbf1\}
\]
be the elliptope.  The full arcsine expression is tautologically exact:
\begin{equation}
 M(A)=\max_{R\in\cE_n}
 \left|\frac2\pi\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}\arcsin R_{ij}\right|.
 \label{eq:arcsineexact}
\end{equation}
Indeed, Gaussian sign rounding makes every value on the right at most
\(M(A)\), while \(R=xx^{\mathsf T}\) attains \(Q_A(x)\).  The nonlinearity
therefore preserves the original problem rather than relaxing it.

Linearizing gives a model with a complete thermodynamic limit.  Put
\[
 S(A)=\max_{R\in\cE_n}\left|\frac12\tr(AR)\right|,
 \qquad S_n=\min_A S(A).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Adversarial elliptope limit]
\label{thm:elliptope}
For every \(n\ge2\),
\[
 S_n\ge\frac12n\sqrt{n-1},
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{S_n}{n^{3/2}}=\frac12.}
\]
At each symmetric conference order, equality holds in the finite bound.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix any sign matrix \(A\), set \(q=n-1\), and define
\[
 R^\pm=\frac12\left(I\pm\frac A{\sqrt q}\right)^2.
\]
They are positive semidefinite.  Since every row of \(A\) has squared norm
\(q\), their diagonals equal one.  Moreover
\[
 R^+-R^-=\frac{2A}{\sqrt q}.
\]
The corresponding two linear SDP values differ by
\[
 \frac12\tr\bigl(A(R^+-R^-)\bigr)=n\sqrt q.
\]
At least one has absolute value at least half this amount.

For the reverse asymptotic inequality, if \(R\in\cE_n\), then
\[
 |\tr(AR)|\le\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\tr R=n\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
A symmetric conference matrix has operator norm \(\sqrt{n-1}\), so it
attains equality.  For general \(n\), the prime number theorem in arithmetic
progressions supplies a prime \(q\equiv1\pmod4\) with
\(q+1\ge n\) and \(q+1=n(1+o(1))\) \cite{davenport}.  Take a principal \(n\)-by-\(n\)
submatrix of the symmetric Paley conference matrix of order \(N=q+1\).
Compression does not increase its operator norm \(\sqrt{N-1}\), giving
\(S_n\le n\sqrt{N-1}/2=(1/2+o(1))n^{3/2}\).
\end{proof}

Rank-one \(R=xx^{\mathsf T}\) show \(S(A)\ge M(A)\).  The missing transfer is
an asymptotically vanishing integrality gap, which is not supplied by standard
Grothendieck-type constant-factor rounding \cite{alon-naor,nesterov}.  The gap
is already strict at order six: the exact computation below gives \(F(6)=5\),
whereas Theorem~\ref{thm:elliptope} and a conference matrix give
\(S_6=3\sqrt5\).

There is also a sharp barrier for the linear Gaussian-sign method.  Let
\(\Lambda(A)\) be the maximum of
\[
 \sum_{i<j}a_{ij}(R^+_{ij}-R^-_{ij})
\]
over \(R^\pm\in\cE_n\), subject to the edgewise orientations
\(a_{ij}(R^+_{ij}-R^-_{ij})\ge0\).

\begin{theorem}[Oriented covariance barrier]
\label{thm:lambda}
For every sign matrix \(A\),
\[
 M(A)\ge\frac{\Lambda(A)}\pi,
 \qquad
 \Lambda(A)\ge n\sqrt{n-1}.
\]
Moreover
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \lim_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{\min_A\Lambda(A)}{n^{3/2}}=1.}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Gaussian sign rounding of \(R^+\) and \(R^-\), followed by the arcsine law,
gives two expected energies.  Since \(\arcsin'(r)\ge1\) and each edge
difference has the prescribed orientation, their difference is at least
\(2\Lambda(A)/\pi\).  Both expectations lie in \([-M(A),M(A)]\), proving the
first inequality.

The square covariances used in Theorem~\ref{thm:elliptope} satisfy the
orientation constraints and have objective \(n\sqrt{n-1}\).  Conversely,
\[
 \Lambda(A)=\frac12\tr\bigl(A(R^+-R^-)\bigr)
 \le n\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Conference matrices attain equality.  The same prime-number-theorem and Paley
principal-submatrix construction used in Theorem~\ref{thm:elliptope} gives the
matching asymptotic upper bound at every order.
\end{proof}

Thus the optimized two-covariance, edgewise-oriented linear certificate
\(\Lambda\) defined above has adversarial asymptotic lower-bound constant
exactly \(1/\pi\).  Improving it requires information beyond this specific
linearization; the theorem does not exclude more general covariance
certificates.

\section{The augmented cut code and its exact dual}

Encode signs as bits via \((-1)^b\).  Let \(j\in\Ftwo^m\) be the all-one edge
word and
\[
 (\delta u)_{ij}=u_i+u_j.
\]
Then the augmented code in \eqref{eq:oldfacts} is
\[
 D_n=\{\delta u+s j:u\in\Ftwo^n,\ s\in\Ftwo\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Linear code and dual]
\label{thm:codedual}
For \(n\ge3\), \(D_n\) is a binary \([m,n]\) linear code and
\[
 D_n^\perp=
 \{H\subseteq E(K_n):
 \deg_H(v)\equiv0\pmod2\ \text{for all }v,
 \ |H|\equiv0\pmod2\}.
\]
For \(n\ge4\), the dual is spanned by 4-cycles, has dimension \(m-n\), and
minimum distance four.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The cut space has dimension \(n-1\).  Its words have even parity on every
triangle, whereas \(j\) has odd triangle parity, so adjoining \(j\) raises the
dimension by one.  A graph is orthogonal to every cut exactly when every
vertex has even degree, and it is orthogonal to \(j\) exactly when its number
of edges is even.

The cycle space is generated by triangles through one fixed vertex.  An
even-weight cycle-space word is a sum of an even number of these basis
triangles.  The sum of two sharing a nonfixed vertex is a 4-cycle.  Two that
share only the fixed vertex can be paired through an intermediate basis
triangle and expressed as two 4-cycles.  This proves generation.  A nonzero
even-cardinality Eulerian graph has at least four edges, and a 4-cycle exists.
\end{proof}

Counting cuts by the size of one shore gives
\begin{equation}
 W_{D_n}(z)=\frac12\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk
 \left(z^{k(n-k)}+z^{m-k(n-k)}\right).
 \label{eq:weightenum}
\end{equation}

Let \(a_e=(-1)^{y_e}\) and define the exact augmented partition function
\[
 Z_y(\beta)=\sum_{d\in D_n}
 \exp\bigl(\beta[m-2\wt(y+d)]\bigr).
\]
Its ground state is \(M(A)\).

\begin{theorem}[Signed-Eulerian MacWilliams formula]
\label{thm:macwilliams}
For \(n\ge3\),
\[
 Z_y(\beta)=2^n(\cosh\beta)^mP_y(\tanh\beta),
\]
where
\[
 P_y(t)=\sum_{H\in D_n^\perp}(-1)^{|H\cap y|}t^{|H|}.
\]
For \(0\le t<1\), \(P_y(t)>0\), and uniformly in \(y\),
\[
 M(A)\le\frac1\beta\log Z_y(\beta)
 \le M(A)+\frac{n\log2}\beta.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The coset MacWilliams identity is
\[
 W_{y+D_n}(z)=2^{n-m}(1+z)^m
 \sum_{H\in D_n^\perp}(-1)^{|H\cap y|}
 \left(\frac{1-z}{1+z}\right)^{|H|}.
\]
Multiply by \(e^{\beta m}\) and substitute \(z=e^{-2\beta}\).  This gives
the formula because
\(e^{\beta}(1+e^{-2\beta})=2\cosh\beta\) and the final ratio is
\(\tanh\beta\).  Positivity follows because the left side is a positive
partition sum.  The last display compares its largest summand with a sum of
\(|D_n|=2^n\) summands.
\end{proof}

For fixed \(\beta>0\), the minimized free energy in
Theorem~\ref{thm:macwilliams}, divided by \(n^{3/2}\), differs from
\(F(n)/n^{3/2}\) by \(O(n^{-1/2})\).  Thus the exact unresolved term is
\[
 \min_y\log P_y(\tanh\beta).
\]
It must cancel the explicit \(m\log\cosh\beta\) term to \(n^{3/2}\)
accuracy.  Averaging \(P_y(t)\) over all \(y\) gives one and yields only
\[
 F(n)\le n\sqrt{(n-1)\log2},
\]
the usual sphere/union-bound constant, weaker than \(1/2\).

Puncturing also stops at the old scale.  For \(n\ge4\), deleting the final star
maps \(D_n\) onto \(D_{n-1}\), and the two lifts of each word have complementary
star coordinates.  Therefore
\[
 \rho(D_{n-1})\le\rho(D_n)
 \le\rho(D_{n-1})+\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor,
\]
equivalently
\[
 F(n-1)+((n-1)\bmod2)\le F(n)\le F(n-1)+n-1.
\]
The \(n=3\) instance of these inequalities is checked directly; the fiber
there has four rather than two words.
Across a two-block partition, the cross lifts are complementary rank-one
sign matrices.  On the dual side, the
\(\binom n2\binom k2\) alternating cross-block 4-cycles already contribute
macroscopically at mean-field scaling.  Product bounds that take absolute
values discard the cancellation needed here.

\section{High-temperature data do not truncate uniformly}

Let \(X\) be uniform on \(\eps^n\), let \(Y=Q_A(X)\), and use the symmetrized
partition function \(\E\cosh(tY)\), equivalently the augmented state \(sY\).
Direct Walsh counting gives
\begin{equation}
 \E Y^2=m,
 \qquad
 \E Y^4=3m^2-2m+24\sum_{C_4}\prod_{e\in C_4}a_e,
 \label{eq:fourthmoment}
\end{equation}
where the sum is over undirected 4-cycles.  Indeed, a monomial in the fourth
power survives exactly when every vertex has even incidence.  Besides one
edge four times and two edges twice, the only case is a simple 4-cycle, with
multinomial factor \(24\).

Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_4(Y)=3\tr(A^4)-2n(n-1)(3n-4),
 \qquad
 \tr(A^4)=n(n-1)^2+\sum_{i\ne j}(A^2_{ij})^2.
 \label{eq:tracefour}
\end{equation}
The second identity is \(\tr(A^4)=\sum_{ij}(A^2_{ij})^2\); the first follows
by separating the closed walks with four distinct vertices in
\eqref{eq:fourthmoment}.

The expansion
\[
 \log\E\cosh(tY)=\frac{m t^2}{2}+\frac{\kappa_4(Y)t^4}{24}+O(t^6)
\]
shows that, for each fixed \(n\), all sufficiently high-temperature
minimizers of this symmetrized partition function minimize \(\tr(A^4)\).
The symmetrization matters: the one-sided partition can have a
signing-dependent cubic triangle term.  Spectral Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \tr(A^4)\ge\frac{\tr(A^2)^2}{n}=n(n-1)^2.
\]
For a Paley principal submatrix of order \(n\) inside a conference matrix of
order \(N=n(1+o(1))\),
\[
 \tr(A^4)\le \|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\tr(A^2)
 \le (N-1)n(n-1).
\]
This gives the reverse asymptotic inequality, so
\[
 \lim_n\frac{\min_A\tr(A^4)}{n^3}=1,
 \qquad
 \lim_n\frac{\min_A\kappa_4(Y)}{n^3}=-3.
\]

This fourth-order limit does not determine the next term.  Exhausting all
root-normalized order-seven signings gives
\[
 \min_A\tr(A^4)=342,
\]
while the trace-minimizing signings have three distinct exact sixth moments,
\[
 50{,}781,\qquad 53{,}661,\qquad 59{,}421.
\]
Thus fourth-order data alone are insufficient.  No uniform remainder theorem
connecting this high-temperature expansion to the required ground-state
regime was obtained.

\section{Ordinary graphons and empirical spectra lose the functional}

For a signed matrix \(A\), let \(W_A\) be its step graphon and normalize
\[
 \|W_A\|_\square=\frac1{n^2}
 \max_{S,T\subseteq[n]}
 \left|\sum_{i\in S,j\in T}a_{ij}\right|.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Cut-limit obstruction]
\label{thm:graphon}
If \(M(A_n)=O(n^{3/2})\), then \(W_{A_n}\to0\) in cut norm.  There are two
deterministic sequences satisfying this hypothesis and having the same zero
graphon limit, while their normalized Boolean maxima are separated by at
least \(3/2\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(x,y\in\eps^n\), split the coordinates according to whether \(x_i=y_i\).
Writing \(z^+,z^-\in\{-1,0,1\}^n\) for the corresponding restrictions of
\(y\), symmetry of \(A\) cancels cross terms and gives
\[
 x^{\mathsf T}Ay=2Q_A(z^+)-2Q_A(z^-).
\]
A multilinear quadratic form attains its maximum absolute value on
\([-1,1]^n\) at a Boolean vertex, so every sign bilinear form is bounded by
\(4M(A)\).  Expressing two indicator vectors as affine combinations of sign
vectors then gives
\[
 \|W_A\|_\square\le\frac{4M(A)}{n^2}.
\]

Take an infinite sequence of Paley conference matrices \(C_N\).  Their
normalized Boolean maxima have limsup at most \(1/2\), and their graphons tend
to zero.  Choose \(k=\lfloor2N^{3/4}\rfloor\) vertices and change every
coefficient inside that set to \(+1\), obtaining \(B_N\).  At most
\(\binom k2=O(N^{3/2})\) edges change, so \(M(B_N)=O(N^{3/2})\) and the
graphon perturbation tends to zero in \(L^1\).  Fix the planted spins to
\(+1\) and average all outside spins independently.  Every cross and outside
term has mean zero, while the planted block contributes \(\binom k2\).
Some outside assignment therefore has at least that energy, and
\[
 \liminf_N\frac{M(B_N)}{N^{3/2}}\ge2.
\]
The separation from the conference limsup is at least \(3/2\).
\end{proof}

The usual dense graph topology therefore collapses every target-scale
near-optimizer to the same point.  This is not repaired by keeping only a
limiting empirical spectrum.  At dense scaling, every empirical law of
\(A/n\) tends to \(\delta_0\), since its average squared eigenvalue is
\((n-1)/n^2\).  At fluctuation scaling there is a stronger obstruction.

\begin{proposition}[Empirical-spectral obstruction]
\label{prop:spectrum}
For every fixed \(d>1/2\), there are conference matrices \(C_N\) and sign
matrices \(B_N\) such that
\[
 W_2(\mu_{B_N/\sqrt N},\mu_{C_N/\sqrt N})\longrightarrow0,
\]
but
\[
 \liminf_N\frac{M(B_N)}{N^{3/2}}\ge d,
 \qquad
 \limsup_N\frac{M(C_N)}{N^{3/2}}\le\frac12.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(q_N=Q_{C_N}(\mathbf1)\).  The spectral bound gives
\(|q_N|\le N\sqrt{N-1}/2\).  Flip negative coefficients to positive until
the all-one energy is at least \(dN^{3/2}\).  This needs \(O(N^{3/2})\) edges,
and there are \(\Theta(N^2)\) available.  Therefore
\(\|B_N-C_N\|_F^2=O(N^{3/2})\).  Hoffman--Wielandt implies
\[
 W_2^2(\mu_{B_N/\sqrt N},\mu_{C_N/\sqrt N})
 \le\frac{\|B_N-C_N\|_F^2}{N^2}=O(N^{-1/2}).
\]
The energy inequalities follow from the construction and the conference
spectral bound.
\end{proof}

This excludes dense graphons and limiting empirical spectral distributions,
not spectral edges, eigenvectors, or a richer Gaussian-process limit.

For comparison, iid signings are rigorously worse than the conference upper
constant.

\begin{proposition}[Greedy energy of an iid signing]
On one infinite iid Rademacher edge array \(A_n\), almost surely
\[
 \liminf_n\frac{M(A_n)}{n^{3/2}}
 \ge\frac23\sqrt{\frac2\pi}=0.531923\ldots>\frac12.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose spins sequentially by
\[
 x_1=1,\qquad x_j=\operatorname{sgn}\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}x_i.
\]
The resulting energy is
\[
 Q_{A_n}(x)=\sum_{j=2}^n
 \left|\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}x_i\right|=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}Y_k.
\]
Before column \(j\) is exposed, the earlier spins are measurable with respect
to earlier columns.  Multiplying the fresh iid column signs by those
predictable spins preserves their iid Rademacher law.  Hence the \(Y_k\) are
independent and \(Y_k\) has the law of
\(|\varepsilon_1+\cdots+\varepsilon_k|\).

The central limit theorem and uniform integrability give
\(\E Y_k/\sqrt k\to\sqrt{2/\pi}\).  A Riemann-sum argument therefore yields
\[
 \frac1{n^{3/2}}\sum_{k<n}\E Y_k
 \longrightarrow \sqrt{\frac2\pi}\int_0^1\sqrt t\,dt
 =\frac23\sqrt{\frac2\pi}.
\]
Changing one input sign changes \(Y_k\) by at most two, so its centered moment
generating function is bounded by \(\exp(\lambda^2k/2)\).  Independence gives
an exponential tail \(2e^{-c_\varepsilon n}\) for deviations of
\(\sum_{k<n}Y_k\) by \(\varepsilon n^{3/2}\).  This is summable; Borel--Cantelli
completes the proof.
\end{proof}

This is not a lower bound on \(F(n)\); it shows that typical iid signings are
not asymptotically optimal.

\section{Finite non-heredity and an exact weighted covering state}

For fixed signings $A$ and $B$, define
\[
 J(A,B)=\min_{C\in\eps^{s\times k}}
 M\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\C^{\mathsf T}&B\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad E(B)=J([0],B).
\]
Complete cross-block enumeration over the optimal switching-permutation
classes through total order ten gives, in particular,
\begin{equation}
 \min_{M(A)=F(2),\ M(B)=F(8)}J(A,B)=15>F(10)=13.
 \label{eq:twoeight}
\end{equation}
Hence no optimal order-10 signing contains an optimal order-8 principal
submatrix.  The smallest possible internal sacrifice repairs the obstruction:
among the 1,044 root-normalized order-8 representatives, internal maximum 12
is necessary and sufficient for a $2+8$ completion of maximum 13.  The same
enumeration gives one-vertex extension distributions
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
n&\text{distribution of $E(B)$ over optimal classes}\\ \hline
7&10^{\times4},\ 12^{\times2}\\
8&12^{\times2}\\
9&13^{\times4},\ 15^{\times11}\\
10&17^{\times1},\ 19^{\times1}.
\end{array}
\]
Thus the scalar $M(B)$ does not determine future composition cost.

There is, however, an exact coding-theoretic state for one-vertex extension.
Let
\[
 \mathcal P_n=\eps^n/\{x\sim-x\},\qquad
 d_\pm([u],[v])=\min\{d_H(u,v),d_H(u,-v)\}.
\]
For an order-$n$ signing $B$, put $M=M(B)$ and
\[
 w_B([x])=\frac{M-|Q_B(x)|}{2},
 \qquad
 \rho_{\mathrm w}(B)=
 \max_{[b]\in\mathcal P_n}\min_{[x]\in\mathcal P_n}
 \bigl(d_\pm([b],[x])+w_B([x])\bigr).
\]
The weights are nonnegative integers because every quadratic energy has the
parity of $\binom n2$.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted covering-radius Bellman identity]
\label{thm:weighted-bellman}
For every order-$n$ signing $B$,
\[
 E(B)=M(B)+n-2\rho_{\mathrm w}(B).
\]
Equivalently, if
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm w}(B)=\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor
 -\rho_{\mathrm w}(B),
\]
then
\[
 F(n+1)=\min_B\left(
 M(B)+(n\bmod2)+2\delta_{\mathrm w}(B)
 \right),
\]
where the minimum is over all order-$n$ signings.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $b\in\eps^n$ be the incident signs and let $t$ be the new spin.  Pairing
$t=1$ and $t=-1$ gives, for every old spin vector $x$,
\[
 \max_{t\in\eps}|Q_B(x)+t\,b\mathbin\cdot x|
 =|Q_B(x)|+|b\mathbin\cdot x|.
\]
Since $|b\mathbin\cdot x|=n-2d_\pm([b],[x])$, the maximum over $x$ for
fixed $b$ is
\[
 M+n-2\min_{[x]\in\mathcal P_n}
 \bigl(w_B([x])+d_\pm([b],[x])\bigr).
\]
Minimizing over $[b]$ proves the first identity.  The equality
$n-2\lfloor n/2\rfloor=n\bmod2$ gives the deficit form.  Finally,
minimizing over all $B$ and $b$ enumerates every order-$(n+1)$ signing.
\end{proof}

For every center $[b]$, some exact maximizer has projective distance at most
$\lfloor n/2\rfloor$.  A configuration of weight greater than this can never
attain the inner minimum.  Consequently $E(B)$ depends only on the exact
near-ground window
\[
 |Q_B(x)|\ge M(B)-2\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor.
\]
The Bellman state has energy width at most $n$; deeper configurations are
rigorously irrelevant to one-vertex extension.

Let $X_B=\{[x]:|Q_B(x)|=M(B)\}$ and let $\rho_{\rm ext}(B)$ be its
ordinary covering radius in $\mathcal P_n$.  The same proof, restricted to
the maximizing configurations, gives the exact lower obstruction
\[
 E(B)\ge M(B)+n-2\rho_{\rm ext}(B),
 \qquad \rho_{\mathrm w}(B)\le\rho_{\rm ext}(B).
\]
The exhaustive verifier independently recomputes both radii and the full
extension values.  At order nine, the four extendible classes have
$(\rho_{\rm ext},\rho_{\mathrm w})=(4,4)$, while the eleven failing classes
have $(3,3)$.  The order-10 classes have radii $(3,3)$ and $(2,2)$, exactly
explaining extension values 17 and 19.  Exact maximizers are not a complete
state: all six order-7 classes have $\rho_{\rm ext}=3$, but the $|Q_B|=7$
layer lowers $\rho_{\mathrm w}$ to 2 in precisely the two classes with
$E(B)=12$.  This is a finite counterexample to any cavity rule retaining only
the ground-state code.  The full energy-weighted projective landscape is
exact for one step.

The scalar partition function is also insufficient.  The two optimal
order-nine graph6 records \texttt{G?qmaw} and \texttt{GCpbaw} have the same
complete projective absolute-energy histogram
\[
 \#\{|Q_B|=0,4,8,12\}=(60,111,60,25),
\]
and therefore have identical values of
$\sum_xe^{t|Q_B(x)|}$ and $\sum_{x,s}e^{tsQ_B(x)}$ for every real $t$.
Nevertheless their extremizer covering radii are 4 and 3, and direct
enumeration gives extension values 13 and 15.  Thus even the entire scalar
free-energy curve loses the geometry required by a Bellman step.  This does
not refute a nonlocal minimax interpolation with a richer order parameter.

The verifier also exhausts all root-normalized residual graphs through order
eight.  If $\mathcal B_n$ is the coordinatewise Pareto-minimal set of pairs
$(M(B),\delta_{\mathrm w}(B))$, then
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
n&\mathcal B_n\\ \hline
2&\{(1,1)\}\\
3&\{(3,0)\}\\
4&\{(4,0)\}\\
5&\{(4,0)\}\\
6&\{(5,2),(7,1),(9,0)\}\\
7&\{(9,0)\}\\
8&\{(10,1),(12,0)\}.
\end{array}
\]
Every point at order six gives $F(7)=9$, and both points at order eight give
$F(9)=12$.  Thus two units of additional internal energy can be exactly
offset by one unit of covering-deficit improvement.  This is finite evidence
for a near-optimal Pareto family, not an asymptotic conclusion.

\subsection{The nonlinear square-covariance gain}

The earlier Gaussian proof used only the inequality $\arcsin'(u)\ge1$.
Retaining the discarded nonlinear term gives a stability theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Nonlinear Gaussian-sign bound]
\label{thm:nonlinear-gaussian}
For $s\ge0$, put
\[
 D_s=1+s^2(n-1),\qquad d_s=\frac{2s}{D_s},\qquad
 z_{ij,s}=\frac{s^2|(A^2)_{ij}|}{D_s}.
\]
Then every order-$n$ signing satisfies
\[
 M(A)\ge\frac1\pi\sum_{i<j}
 \left[\arcsin(z_{ij,s}+d_s)-\arcsin(z_{ij,s}-d_s)\right].
 \tag{25}
\]
Consequently
\[
 M(A)\ge\frac{n(n-1)}\pi\arcsin\frac1{\sqrt{n-1}},
 \tag{26}
\]
and, for $n\ge3$,
\[
 M(A)\ge\frac{n(n-1)}\pi\arcsin\frac1{\sqrt{n-1}}
 +\frac{\operatorname{tr}(A^4)-n(n-1)^2}
 {8\pi(n-1)(n-2)^{3/2}}.
 \tag{27}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The two matrices
\[
 R_s^\pm=\frac{(I\pm sA)^2}{D_s}
\]
are correlation matrices.  With
$u_{ij}=s^2a_{ij}(A^2)_{ij}/D_s$, the corresponding Gaussian sign
roundings and the arcsine law give
\[
 \mathbb E Q_A(X^+)-\mathbb E Q_A(X^-)
 =\frac2\pi\sum_{i<j}
 [\arcsin(u_{ij}+d_s)-\arcsin(u_{ij}-d_s)].
\]
The bracket is even in $u_{ij}$, and each expectation belongs to
$[-M(A),M(A)]$, proving (25).

For $f_d(z)=\arcsin(z+d)-\arcsin(z-d)$ and $z\ge0$,
\[
 f_d''(z)=h(z+d)-h(z-d),\qquad
 h(t)=\frac{t}{(1-t^2)^{3/2}}.
\]
Thus $f_d$ is even and convex.  At $s=1/\sqrt{n-1}$ one has
$D_s=2$ and $d_s=1/\sqrt{n-1}$, so
$f_d(z)\ge f_d(0)=2\arcsin d$, which proves (26).

Write $q=n-1$.  Since
\[
 h'(t)=\frac{1+2t^2}{(1-t^2)^{5/2}}
\]
is even and increasing in $|t|$, $f_d''(z)$ is increasing for $z\ge0$.
For $d=1/\sqrt q$,
\[
 f_d(z)\ge2\arcsin d+\frac{q}{(q-1)^{3/2}}z^2.
\]
Finally $z_{ij}=|(A^2)_{ij}|/(2q)$ and
\[
 \sum_{i<j}z_{ij}^2
 =\frac{\operatorname{tr}(A^4)-nq^2}{8q^2},
\]
which proves (27).
\end{proof}

The excess is exactly
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(A^4)-n(n-1)^2
 =\|A^2-(n-1)I\|_F^2.
\]
Hence a sequence attaining the asymptotic lower constant $1/\pi$ must obey
$\|A_n^2-(n-1)I\|_F=o(n^2)$.  This is vanishing normalized Gram defect, not a
conference-rigidity conclusion: a uniform random signing already satisfies
this normalization in probability.  The universal improvement in (26) is only
$\Theta(\sqrt n)$, so it does not change the leading constant.  It does imply
the new exact consequence
\[
 \boxed{F(21)\ge32}.
\]
Indeed (26) is greater than 30 and all order-21 energies are even.  For an
exact strictness certificate, use $\pi/14<11/49$ and
$\sin t<t-t^3/6+t^5/120$; rational squaring shows that the latter polynomial
at $11/49$ has square less than $1/20$.  Thus
$\arcsin(1/\sqrt{20})>\pi/14$.

\subsection{The multivertex Bellman state and its entropy bound}

For internal signings $B,D$ of orders $n,k$, define
\[
 J(B,D)=\min_C M\begin{pmatrix}B&C\\C^{\mathsf T}&D\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad L(B,D)=\max_{x,y}|Q_B(x)+Q_D(y)|.
\]
In the projective Hamming cube
$\mathcal P_{nk}=\{\pm1\}^{n\times k}/\{C\sim-C\}$, let
\[
 \mathcal R_{n,k}=\{[xy^{\mathsf T}]:x\in\eps^n,y\in\eps^k\}
\]
and give $R=[xy^{\mathsf T}]$ the integer weight
\[
 w_{B,D}(R)=\frac{L(B,D)-|Q_B(x)+Q_D(y)|}{2}.
\]
Put
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm w}^{\square}(B,D)
 =\max_{[C]\in\mathcal P_{nk}}\min_{R\in\mathcal R_{n,k}}
 [d_\pm([C],R)+w_{B,D}(R)].
\]

\begin{theorem}[Multivertex weighted Bellman identity]
\label{thm:multivertex-bellman}
One has
\[
 J(B,D)=L(B,D)+nk-2\rho_{\mathrm w}^{\square}(B,D).
 \tag{28}
\]
Thus, with
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm w}^{\square}
 =\left\lfloor\frac{nk}{2}\right\rfloor
 -\rho_{\mathrm w}^{\square},
\]
\[
 F(n+k)=\min_{B,D}
 [L(B,D)+(nk\bmod2)+2\delta_{\mathrm w}^{\square}(B,D)].
 \tag{29}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Pairing $y$ with $-y$ gives
\[
 \max_{\eta=\pm1}|Q_B(x)+Q_D(y)+\eta x^{\mathsf T}Cy|
 =|Q_B(x)+Q_D(y)|+|x^{\mathsf T}Cy|.
\]
Moreover
$|x^{\mathsf T}Cy|=nk-2d_\pm([C],[xy^{\mathsf T}])$.
Substitute $|Q_B+Q_D|=L-2w$, maximize over the rank-one code, and minimize
over $[C]$ to obtain (28).  A weight-zero rank-one word is within projective
distance $\lfloor nk/2\rfloor$ of every center, so the deficit is
nonnegative.  Minimizing over the two internal blocks proves (29).
\end{proof}

Only the internal window
\[
 |Q_B(x)+Q_D(y)|\ge L(B,D)-2\left\lfloor\frac{nk}{2}\right\rfloor
\]
can affect this radius.  Define its soft effective size by
\[
 \Xi(B,D)=\log\sum_{R\in\mathcal R_{n,k}}
 \exp\left(-\frac{2w_{B,D}(R)^2}{nk}\right).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Weighted entropy upper bound]
\label{prop:weighted-entropy}
\[
 J(B,D)\le L(B,D)
 +\sqrt{2nk(\Xi(B,D)+\log4)}.
 \tag{30}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose $C$ uniformly at random.  Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound give
\[
 \Pr\{\exists R:\ |\langle C,R\rangle|>T+2w(R)\}
 \le2\exp\left(-\frac{T^2}{2nk}+\Xi(B,D)\right).
\]
For $T=\sqrt{2nk(\Xi+\log4)}$ this is at most $1/2$.  Hence some $C$
satisfies every displayed inequality, and
$L-2w(R)+|\langle C,R\rangle|\le L+T$ for every $R$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:multivertex-bellman} finishes the proof.
\end{proof}

The generic bound $\Xi\le(n+k-2)\log2$ reproduces the known leading
$\sqrt{nk(n+k)}$ block cost.  The initially proposed replacement was the
following constant-matching profile law:
\[
 L(B,D)+\sqrt{2nk(\Xi(B,D)+\log4)}
 \le(F(n)^{2/3}+F(k)^{2/3})^{3/2}
 +O((n+k)^{3/2-\varepsilon}).
 \tag{31}
\]
This would imply power-saving near-subadditivity of $F^{2/3}$, which the
standard blocking argument turns into convergence.  The next proposition
shows that this initially proposed target is actually impossible; it is
retained here as an audited failed route.

There is also density-one control of the exact one-vertex state.  Choose
$B_n$ attaining Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted-bellman} and put
$\sigma_n=M(B_n)-F(n)$ and $\delta_n=\delta_{\mathrm w}(B_n)$.  Exactly
\[
 F(n+1)-F(n)=\sigma_n+(n\bmod2)+2\delta_n.
\]
Telescoping and $F(n)=O(n^{3/2})$ show
\[
 \sum_{n=N}^{2N}(\sigma_n+2\delta_n)=O(N^{3/2}).
\]
Thus, for every $g(N)\to\infty$, all but $O(N/g(N))$ indices in $[N,2N]$
have a Bellman-optimal predecessor with
$\sigma_n\le g(N)\sqrt N$ and
$\delta_n\le\frac12g(N)\sqrt N$.  The scalar countermodel below shows that
even replacing this density-one statement by uniform $O(\sqrt n)$ control
would not prove convergence.

\subsection{Parity gain and the sharp limit of the Gaussian certificate}

Put
\[
 \Delta(A)=\|A^2-(n-1)I\|_F^2
 =\sum_{i\ne j}(A^2)_{ij}^2.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Parity of the Gram defect]
\label{thm:parity-defect}
Every order-$n$ signing satisfies
\[
 \Delta(A)\ge
 \begin{cases}
 n(n-1),&n\text{ odd},\\
 2n(n-2),&n\equiv0\pmod4,\\
 0,&n\equiv2\pmod4.
 \end{cases}
\]
These bounds are attained on infinite Paley-derived subsequences.  In
particular,
\[
 \boxed{F(20)\ge30}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For odd $n$, every off-diagonal entry of $A^2$ is a sum of the odd number
$n-2$ of signs and therefore has square at least one.

Now let $n\equiv0\pmod4$, write $a_{ij}=(-1)^{e_{ij}}$, and put
$d_i=\sum_{j\ne i}e_{ij}\pmod2$.  Reduction modulo four gives
\[
 \frac{(A^2)_{ij}}2\equiv1+d_i+d_j\pmod2.
\]
Thus $|(A^2)_{ij}|\ge2$ for every pair with $d_i=d_j$.  If $r$ of the
$d_i$ are odd, the handshake lemma makes $r$ even and
\[
 \Delta(A)\ge
 8\left[\binom r2+\binom{n-r}2\right]\ge2n(n-2).
\]
Conference matrices show that the third bound can vanish.  Deleting one
vertex from a conference matrix makes all off-diagonal Gram entries signs;
deleting two makes exactly $n(n-2)/2$ ordered entries have magnitude two.
This proves sharpness on the corresponding Paley orders.

For $n=20$, Theorem~\ref{thm:nonlinear-gaussian} and
$\Delta\ge720$ give
\[
 M(A)\ge\frac{380}{\pi}\arcsin\frac1{\sqrt{19}}
 +\frac{90}{19\pi\,18^{3/2}}>28.
\]
For exact strictness, take $x=39/170<1/\sqrt{19}$, use
$1/\sqrt{18}>70/297$, $\arcsin x>x+x^3/6$, and $\pi<22/7$.  The
remaining rational margin is
\[
 380\left(x+\frac{x^3}{6}\right)
 +\frac5{19}\frac{70}{297}-88
 =\frac{8798941}{2772405900}>0.
\]
Order-20 energies are even, so $M(A)\ge30$.
\end{proof}

The full parameter family admits an exact Jensen compression.  With
$q=n-1$, $\varepsilon=\Delta(A)/(nq^3)$, and $0\le w<1$, put
\[
 a=\frac{1+w}{2}\sqrt\varepsilon,\qquad
 d=\frac{\sqrt{1-w^2}}{\sqrt q}.
\]
Then
\[
 M(A)\ge\frac{nq}{2\pi}
 [\arcsin(a+d)-\arcsin(a-d)].
\]
Indeed, after writing $w=(qs^2-1)/(qs^2+1)$, the edge offsets in (25)
have mean square $a^2$.  For fixed $d$, the function
\[
 u\longmapsto
 \arcsin(\sqrt u+d)-\arcsin(\sqrt u-d)
\]
is convex.  To see this, if $f_d(z)$ denotes the arcsine difference, then
\[
 \frac{d^2}{du^2}f_d(\sqrt u)
 =\frac{zf_d''(z)-f_d'(z)}{4z^3}.
\]
The numerator vanishes at zero and has derivative $zf_d'''(z)\ge0$,
because $h'(v)$ is even and increasing in $|v|$ for
$h(v)=v(1-v^2)^{-3/2}$.  The number $a$ is the root-mean-square of the
edge offsets, so it remains in the correlation domain.  Jensen proves the
claim; endpoints follow by continuity.  Consequently, if
$\varepsilon(A_n)\to\varepsilon<1$,
\[
 \liminf_n\frac{M(A_n)}{n^{3/2}}
 \ge\frac1\pi\,
 \sqrt{\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}}}.
\]
For fixed $w$, the limiting factor before optimization is
\[
 \frac1\pi
 \sqrt{\frac{1-w^2}{1-\varepsilon(1+w)^2/4}}.
\]
Here the square root multiplies $1/\pi$; optimizing its square gives
$1+w=2/(1+\sqrt{1-\varepsilon})$ and the preceding formula.

\begin{proposition}[Sharp barrier for the square-covariance family]
\label{prop:nonlinear-barrier}
If $B_s(A)$ is the right side of (25) and
$G_n=\min_A\sup_{s\ge0}B_s(A)$, then
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{G_n}{n^{3/2}}=\frac1\pi.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Taking $s=1/\sqrt{n-1}$ proves the lower bound.  For the reverse inequality,
take an order-$n$ principal block of a Paley conference matrix of order
$n+r$, where $r=o(n)$.  If $U$ is the deleted block, then
$|(A^2)_{ij}|=|(UU^{\mathsf T})_{ij}|\le r$.  Uniformly in $s$,
\[
 z_{ij,s}\le\frac{r}{n-1}=o(1),\qquad
 d_s\le\frac1{\sqrt{n-1}}.
\]
Writing each arcsine difference as an integral bounds it by
$2d_s(1+o(1))$, uniformly in $s$.  Summation gives
$\sup_sB_s(A)\le(1/\pi+o(1))n^{3/2}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Failure of the weighted union bound and an exact orbit state}

\begin{proposition}[Universal entropy floor]
\label{prop:entropy-floor}
For every pair $B,D$,
\[
 L+\sqrt{2nk(\Xi+\log4)}
 \ge\sqrt{2nk(n+k)\log2}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $|\mathcal R_{n,k}|=2^{n+k-2}$ and $0\le w(R)\le L/2$,
\[
 \Xi\ge(n+k-2)\log2-\frac{L^2}{2nk}.
\]
Put $b=\sqrt{2nk(n+k)\log2}$.  If $L\le b$, the left side is at least
$L+\sqrt{b^2-L^2}\ge b$; if $L>b$, the result is immediate.
\end{proof}

For balanced blocks the floor is
$2\sqrt{\log2}\,N^{3/2}$, whereas the desired right side in (31) is at
most $(\sqrt2+o(1))N^{3/2}$.  Thus (31) fails universally by the leading
constant $2\sqrt{\log2}-\sqrt2=0.250895\ldots$.  Even retaining the
discarded cross term in the same Hoeffding sum cannot help: for $K\ge L$,
\[
 2\sum_R\exp\left(-\frac{(K-h(R))^2}{2nk}\right)
 \ge2^{n+k-1}\exp\left(-\frac{K^2}{2nk}\right).
\]

There is an exact dependence-sensitive replacement.  Regard
$G=\mathcal R_{n,k}$ as a group under entrywise multiplication.  For a cross
seed $C_0$ put
\[
 c(U)=|\langle C_0,U\rangle|,\qquad
 h(R)=|Q_B(x)+Q_D(y)|.
\]
The row-and-column switching orbit $C_g=C_0\odot g$ satisfies
\[
 J_{\rm orb}(C_0;B,D)
 =\min_{g\in G}\max_{R\in G}[h(R)+c(gR)].
\]
For $K\ge L$, let $C_t=\{U:c(U)>t\}$.  Its bad shifts are exactly
\[
 \mathcal B_K=\bigcup_{R\in G}R\,C_{K-h(R)}.
\]
Hence the orbit succeeds if and only if $\mathcal B_K\ne G$.  This weighted
sumset noncoverage problem on $\Ftwo^{n+k-2}$ retains the intersections lost
by the union bound.

\subsection{The exact scalar Bellman wall}

Let
\[
 r_n=F(n+1)-F(n)-(n\bmod2)=\sigma_n+2\delta_n.
\]
The normalized dyadic cost
\[
 A_N=N^{-3/2}\sum_{n=N}^{2N-1}r_n
\]
is an exact diagnostic:
\[
 \frac{F(n)}{n^{3/2}}\text{ converges}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A_N\text{ converges}.
\]
If $A_N\to\Lambda$, the limit is
$\Lambda/(2^{3/2}-1)$.  Indeed, with
$c_N=F(N)/N^{3/2}$ and $q=2^{-3/2}$,
\[
 c_{2N}=qc_N+qA_N+O(N^{-1/2}).
\]
The forward implication is immediate.  Conversely, for arbitrary $n$ and
$m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, the same relation and
$F(2m+1)-F(2m)=O(m)$ give
$c_n=q(c_m+\Lambda)+o(1)$.  If
$C=\Lambda/(2^{3/2}-1)$ and $e_n=c_n-C$, boundedness yields
$\limsup|e_n|\le q\limsup|e_n|$, hence convergence at all orders.

Uniform magnitude control of $r_n$ is not enough.  Define
\[
 g(t)=t^{3/2}\left[
 \frac25+\frac1{25}\sin\log\log(t+e^2)\right],
\]
use
\[
 f(1),\ldots,f(10)=0,1,3,4,4,5,9,10,12,13,
\]
and for $n\ge10$ let $f(n+1)-f(n)$ be the least positive integer congruent
to $n$ modulo two and at least $g(n+1)-g(n)$.  Since
\[
 0.52\sqrt t\le g'(t)\le0.68\sqrt t\qquad(t\ge10),
\]
the resulting Bellman residual is nonnegative, even, and at most
$3\sqrt n$.  Moreover
$f(n)=13+g(n)-g(10)+O(n)$, so its normalized liminf and limsup are
$0.36$ and $0.44$: the phase $\log\log(n+e^2)$ is unbounded and its
successive increments tend to zero.  For $n\ge10$,
\[
 f(n)\ge13+g(n)-g(10)
 \ge0.36n^{3/2}+13-0.44\,10^{3/2}
 >\frac{n^{3/2}}{\pi},
\]
and the finite prefix is direct.  The increment ceiling gives the trivial
upper bound.  Positive tail increments, together with the finite prefix,
also give both scalar hereditary consequences proved earlier; the complete
inequality check is in \texttt{RESEARCH\_CONTINUATION.md}.  Thus no theorem
using only those scalar facts can force convergence.  A sufficient
replacement is
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{n\le N}\frac{r_n}{\sqrt n}\longrightarrow a,
\]
because Abel summation then gives $F(N)/N^{3/2}\to2a/3$.

\section{Negative replicas and exact parameter transport}

For uniform disorder $A$ of order $n$, define
\[
 f_{n,t}(A)=\frac{Z_A(t)}{2^{n+1}(\cosh t)^{m_n}},
 \qquad
 \mathcal G_n(q,t)=\log\mathbb E_A f_{n,t}(A)^{-q}.
\]
The denominator is the annealed mean of $Z_A(t)$.

\begin{theorem}[Negative-replica supermultiplicativity]
\label{thm:negative-replica-super}
For every $n,k\ge1$, $q>0$, and $t\in\mathbb R$,
\[
 \mathcal G_{n+k}(q,t)\ge
 \mathcal G_n(q,t)+\mathcal G_k(q,t).
 \tag{NR}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix internal signings $A,B$ and let $C$ be a uniform $n\times k$ cross
signing.  Write
\[
 U_A^\sigma=\sum_xe^{t\sigma Q_A(x)},\qquad
 U_B^\sigma=\sum_ye^{t\sigma Q_B(y)}.
\]
Independent averaging of the cross edges gives
\[
 \mathbb E_CZ_{A,B,C}(t)=(\cosh t)^{nk}P,qquad
 P=U_A^+U_B^++U_A^-U_B^-.
\]
Replacing $B$ by $-B$ gives
$P'=U_A^+U_B^-+U_A^-U_B^+$, and $P+P'=Z_AZ_B$.  Convexity of
$z\mapsto z^{-q}$, first over $C$ and then over the two orientations, yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac12\left(\mathbb E_CZ_{A,B,C}^{-q}
 +\mathbb E_CZ_{A,-B,C}^{-q}\right)\\
&\quad\ge\frac{(\cosh t)^{-qnk}}2(P^{-q}+{P'}^{-q})\\
&\quad\ge2^q(\cosh t)^{-qnk}(Z_AZ_B)^{-q}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Uniform disorder is invariant under $B\mapsto-B$.  After averaging $A,B$,
the factors $2^q$ and $(\cosh t)^{-qnk}$ cancel exactly against the
normalizers because $m_{n+k}=m_n+m_k+nk$.  This proves (NR).  At $t=0$ there
is equality.
\end{proof}

Put $u=\tanh t$ and write
$f_{n,u}=f_{n,\operatorname{arctanh}u}$ and
$\mathcal G_n(q,u)=\mathcal G_n(q,\operatorname{arctanh}u)$.  The signed even-Eulerian expansion
above gives
\[
 f_{n,u}=T_{u/v}f_{n,v}\qquad(0<u<v<1),
\]
where $T_\rho$ is the Boolean noise operator on disorder space.  Sharp
reverse hypercontractivity for positive functions gives, when $Q'>Q>0$,
\[
 \|T_\rho f\|_{-Q'}\ge\|f\|_{-Q},
 \qquad \rho\le\sqrt{\frac{1+Q}{1+Q'}}
 \cite{borell-reverse}.
\]
Since $\|f\|_{-Q}=\exp[-\mathcal G_n(Q,u)/Q]$ in this parametrization, on the exact invariant curve
$(1+Q')u'^2=(1+Q)u^2$ this says
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\mathcal G_n(Q',u')}{Q'}
 \le\frac{\mathcal G_n(Q,u)}Q.
 \label{eq:reverse-transport}
\end{equation}
This is precisely the curve produced by splitting an extensive system, but
the inequality points in the wrong direction for closing (NR).

Fix $\beta,\theta>0$ and, for sufficiently large $r$, set
\[
 t_r=\frac\beta{\sqrt r},\qquad
 q_r=\frac\theta{\tanh^2t_r}-1
 =\frac\theta{\beta^2}r+\frac{2\theta}{3}-1+O(r^{-1}).
\]

\begin{conjecture}[Power-saving negative-replica transport]
\label{conj:transport}
For every $0<\varepsilon<1/2$ there are $C,\delta>0$ such that, whenever
$\varepsilon N\le n\le(1-\varepsilon)N$,
\[
 0\le\frac{q_N}{q_n}\mathcal G_n(q_n,t_n)
 -\mathcal G_n(q_N,t_N)\le CN^{2-\delta}.
 \tag{PT}
\]
\end{conjecture}

Only the first inequality is proved by
\eqref{eq:reverse-transport}.  If the upper estimate held and
$a_n=\mathcal G_n(q_n,t_n)/q_n$, Theorem~\ref{thm:negative-replica-super}
would give
\[
 a_{n+k}\ge a_n+a_k-O((n+k)^{1-\delta})
\]
on balanced splits.

\begin{lemma}[Balanced almost-superadditivity]
\label{lem:balanced-superadditivity}
Let $b_n\ge0$ with $b_n=O(n)$.  If, for some $K,\delta>0$ and every
sufficiently large balanced split $N=j+k$,
\[
 b_N\ge b_j+b_k-KN^{1-\delta},
 \qquad N/3\le j,k\le2N/3,
\]
then $b_n/n$ converges.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix a sufficiently large $m$.  Write $N=qm+r$, $0\le r<m$.  If $r>0$,
partition $N$ into $q-1$ leaves of weight $m$ and one leaf of weight $m+r$;
if $r=0$, use $q$ leaves of weight $m$.  These weights lie in $[m,2m)$.
Recursively splitting the number of leaves as evenly as possible, placing the
single heavier leaf on the smaller side when needed, gives a binary tree in
which each child has between one third and two thirds of its parent's total
weight.

At an internal node of weight $W$, charge its error $KW^{1-\delta}$ to
descendant leaves proportionally to their weights.  Parent weights grow by a
factor at least $3/2$ along each upward path, so the charge per unit leaf
weight is at most
\[
 K\sum_{h\ge0}((3/2)^hm)^{-\delta}=O(m^{-\delta}).
\]
The total accumulated error is therefore $O(Nm^{-\delta})$.  Dropping the
nonnegative contribution of the one exceptional leaf gives
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{b_N}{N}
 \ge \frac{b_m}{m}-O(m^{-\delta}).
\]
Taking $m$ to infinity along a subsequence attaining
$\limsup b_m/m$ proves the claim.
\end{proof}

Here $0\le a_n\le m_n\log\cosh t_n=O_\beta(n)$, so the lemma proves
convergence of $a_n/n$.  The exact soft-minimum error is asymptotically at
most $\beta\log2/(2\theta)$.  Thus (PT) for unbounded $\theta$ at every fixed
$\beta$, and for unbounded $\beta$, would settle the original question.

The finite evidence is adverse but not decisive.  Exact energy spectra and
exact labeled class multiplicities through order nine give
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
n&4&5&6&7&8&9\\ \hline
\Delta_n/n^2,\ (\beta,\theta)=(1,1)
&.00492&.00878&.01166&.01387&.01560&.01700\\
\Delta_n/n^2,\ (\beta,\theta)=(2,4)
&.14850&.27713&.32759&.28609&.30762&.31597,
\end{array}
\]
where
$\Delta_n=(q_{2n}/q_n)\mathcal G_n(q_n,t_n)
-\mathcal G_n(q_{2n},t_{2n})$.  These values neither disprove (PT) nor show
an asymptotic gap, but they do not support saturation.

\section{A known square-order Paley ceiling theorem}

The following classical regular-conference construction corrects an
overstated external report.  It is not a new result
\cite{seidel-two-graphs,goryainov-deza}.

\begin{theorem}[Regular Paley Boolean eigenvector]
\label{thm:paley-boolean}
Let $m$ be an odd prime power.  A Paley conference matrix $C$ of order
$m^2+1$ has a Boolean eigenvector of eigenvalue $m$, and
\[
 M(C)=\frac{m(m^2+1)}2.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $K=\mathbb F_{m^2}$, $H=\mathbb F_m$, and let $\chi$ be the quadratic
character of $K$.  Index $C$ by $\{\infty\}\cup K$, with
$C_{\infty,a}=1$ and $C_{a,b}=\chi(a-b)$.  Choose signs
$\sigma:K/H\to\eps$ with $(m+1)/2$ positive cosets, so
$\sum_c\sigma(c)=1$, and put $x_\infty=1$, $x_a=\sigma(a+H)$.

Every element of $H^*$ is a square in $K$, so the character sum on the zero
additive coset is $m-1$.  Multiplication by $H^*$ is transitive on the
nonzero cosets of the one-dimensional quotient $K/H$ and preserves $\chi$.
All nonzero-coset sums are therefore equal; the total character sum on $K$ is
zero, so each equals $-1$.  For $a$ in coset $c$,
\[
 (Cx)_a=1+(m-1)\sigma(c)-\sum_{d\ne c}\sigma(d)=m\sigma(c),
 \qquad (Cx)_\infty=m.
\]
Thus $Cx=mx$.  The conference identity $C^2=m^2I$ supplies the matching
spectral upper bound.
\end{proof}

These full Paley matrices have normalized Boolean maximum tending to $1/2$.
This gives no lower bound on the minimax $F(m^2+1)$.  Already $m=3$ gives
$M(C)=15$ at order ten, while $F(10)=13$.  The theorem neither improves the
known upper bound nor excludes better signings or principal submatrices.

\section{Six proof programs and the sharp remaining lemma}

The detailed derivations and failed variants are preserved in
\texttt{RESEARCH\_LEDGER.md}.  The current programs are logically distinct.

\paragraph{A. Quenched extensive free energy.}
Prove \(\lim_n\Psi_{n,\beta}\) for every fixed \(\beta>0\), or at least for
an unbounded set of \(\beta\)'s.  Equation~\eqref{eq:extensivesandwich} then
forces convergence.  Theorem~\ref{thm:negative-replica-super} gives an exact
outer-minimum-compatible recursion, but Conjecture~\ref{conj:transport} is the
missing parameter comparison and its finite data are adverse.  Either proving
that power-saving transport for unbounded parameters or proving a leading
transport gap would materially decide this branch.  Proposition
\ref{prop:blocktemp} separately shows why independent annealed cross blocks
fail.  A repair must absorb the cross term in a joint minimax order parameter,
as random-disorder spin-glass interpolation absorbs it only after disorder
expectation \cite{guerra-toninelli,auffinger-chen}.

\paragraph{B. Near-saturated relaxation.}
Find a nonconvex compact relaxation whose optimizers have
\(\sum_e(1-a_e^2)=o(n^2)\) and an asymptotic composition law.  Then
Proposition~\ref{prop:rounding} transfers its limit to signs.  The cube and
sphere calculations prove that the two obvious feasible sets are structurally
too loose or exactly discrete.

\paragraph{C. Signed Eulerian polynomial.}
Prove a second-order limit for
\(\min_y\log P_y(t)\) after the explicit MacWilliams terms are included.
Puncturing and ordinary products fail because of the common parity phase,
rank-one cross lift, and macroscopic family of mixed 4-cycles.  A quenched
coset-weight theorem, rather than an average weight enumerator, is required.

\paragraph{D. Cavity covering profile.}
Control the Pareto set $(M(B),\delta_{\mathrm w}(B))$ uniformly in the
near-optimal energy window.  Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted-bellman} makes this
state exact for one vertex, while the order-seven computation rules out the
smaller state consisting only of exact maximizers.  A useful theorem must
either stabilize the normalized dyadic Bellman cost or prove switching-orbit
noncoverage for the multivertex rank-one group.  Proposition
\ref{prop:entropy-floor} rules out the all-state weighted union bound, so the
replacement must exploit intersections among bad constraints.

\paragraph{E. Second-order limit object.}
Construct a rate-sensitive topology for \(A/\sqrt n\) in which the Boolean
maximum is continuous and sign arrays have an asymptotically lossless
composition.  Theorem~\ref{thm:graphon} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:spectrum} rule out ordinary graphons and empirical
spectra.  Spectral edges, eigenvectors, or the whole induced Gaussian process
would have to remain visible.

\paragraph{F. Nonexistence.}
To separate subsequences one needs order-specific lower bounds on \(F(n)\),
not merely constructions with different energies.  Conference, random, and
planted matrices supply upper bounds or deliberately inferior examples.  The
small values fluctuate but do not certify a subsequence mechanism.

No route presently closes.  The ultimate finite-temperature lemma remains the
following; Conjecture~\ref{conj:transport} is one precise sufficient mechanism
for it, not a proved or empirically favored theorem.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.88\textwidth}
\textbf{Quenched minimax free-energy lemma.}
For every fixed \(\beta>0\), the limit
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\min_A\frac1{\beta n}
 \log\sum_{x,s}\exp\left(\frac{\beta sQ_A(x)}{\sqrt n}\right)
\]
exists.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

Proving this lemma for an unbounded collection of inverse temperatures settles
the MathOverflow question by \eqref{eq:extensivesandwich}.  In coding
coordinates it asks for cancellation in
\(P_y(\tanh(\beta/\sqrt n))\).  Any proof that bounds the mixed cross-block
contribution independently incurs a leading error; a successful proof must
make that contribution part of the optimized variational structure.  The next
negative-replica calculation should therefore determine whether
$\Delta_n/n^2\to0$ or stays bounded away from zero, rather than merely extend
the table of $F(n)$.
